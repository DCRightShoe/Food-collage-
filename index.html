Cultural Crossover: A Celebration of Different Dishes

Food is an important part of culture. It connects people through traditions, history, and shared experiences. When we celebrate the dishes of different cultures, we gain the ability to connect our own experiences with those of others. This website is a celebration of the diversity of food and the stories behind it. In this website, we explore three dishes from different cultural backgrounds, looking at their origins, ingredients, and importance to the people who prepare them.

Dish 1: Kibbeh

Fun Facts About Kibbeh

  • The word "kibbeh" comes from the Arabic "kubbah", meaning "ball".
  • The city of Aleppo in Syria is famous for its incredible variety of kibbeh. There are around 58 different types of kibbeh from the region.
  • In 2009, Lebanon's Almidan NGO made a giant kibbeh weighing 233 kilograms (about 514 pounds), using 120 kilograms of ground meat, 80 kilograms of bulgur, and 80 liters of olive oil. This was the product of the effort of 25 women.
Three Variations of Kibbeh
Kibbeh Nayyeh (Raw)

This is the raw version, similar to steak tartare. It's made from very finely minced high-quality lamb or beef mixed with bulgur and spices. Because it requires fresh, top-quality meat, it is traditionally served to honor guests.

Kibbeh Labaniyeh (In Plain Yogurt)

In this variation, kibbeh balls are cooked and served in a warm, garlicky yogurt sauce, often served over a bed of rice. It's a comforting and popular dish in Lebanon and Syria.

Kibbeh Bil Sanieh (Baked)

This is the "lazy" or casserole version, which is much less time-consuming than shaping individual balls. The kibbeh mixture is layered in a baking dish with a spiced meat filling in the middle, then baked and cut into diamond shapes.

Recipe: Kibbeh Bil Sanieh (Baked Kibbeh)

This is one of the most popular and accessible ways to make kibbeh at home, often served as a main course.

Ingredients
For the Kibbeh Dough (Outer Layer):
  • 1 cup fine bulgur wheat
  • 1.5 cups hot water
  • 500g (about 1 lb) lean ground lamb or beef
  • 1 small onion, quartered
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 tsp seven spices (or a mix of allspice, black pepper, and cumin)
  • Salt to taste
For the Filling (Hashweh):
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 300g (about ⅔ lb) ground lamb or beef
  • 50g pine nuts, toasted
  • 1 tsp seven spices
  • Salt and pepper to taste
Instructions
  1. Soak the Bulgur: Place the fine bulgur in a bowl and cover it with the hot water. Let it sit for about 10–15 minutes until the water is absorbed and the bulgur is tender. Drain any excess water and squeeze it dry.
  2. Make the Filling: While the bulgur soaks, heat the olive oil in a large skillet. Sauté the chopped onion until soft. Add the ground meat and cook until browned. Stir in the toasted pine nuts and seven spices, then season with salt and pepper. Set aside.
  3. Prepare the Kibbeh Dough: In a food processor, combine the soaked bulgur, the quartered onion, and your spices. Pulse until well blended. Add the lean ground meat in batches and pulse until the mixture forms a smooth, paste-like dough. You may need to scrape down the sides.
  4. Assemble the Dish: Preheat your oven to 180°C (350°F) and grease a round or rectangular baking dish with olive oil.
    • Press half of the kibbeh dough evenly into the bottom of the dish (wetting your hands with cold water helps prevent sticking).
    • Spread the meat filling evenly over the first layer.
    • Top with the remaining kibbeh dough, pressing it down gently to seal the edges.
  5. Score and Bake: Using a wet knife, cut a diamond pattern through the top layer of the kibbeh. Drizzle the top with a little olive oil. Bake for 40–50 minutes, until the top is golden brown and cooked through.
  6. Serve: Let it cool for a few minutes before serving. It goes perfectly with a side of yogurt, lentil soup, a fresh salad, or vermicelli rice.
